# Chapter 5: Reading a Stock's "Personality" Before You Trade It

## 5.1 The Daily Chart Context

Before trading any stock intraday, spend 30 seconds on the daily chart. The daily chart tells you the stock's "story" — the context that intraday price action exists within.

**What to look for:**

**Trend:** Is the stock in an uptrend (higher highs, higher lows), downtrend (lower highs, lower lows), or range? A stock gapping up in an established uptrend is more likely to continue than one gapping up in a downtrend (which may be a dead cat bounce).

**Key moving averages:**
- **20 SMA (short-term trend):** If the stock is above the 20 SMA, short-term momentum is bullish. If it's gapping up FROM the 20 SMA, that's a continuation setup
- **50 SMA (medium-term trend):** Institutional reference. Stocks above the 50 SMA get more buying interest. A stock gapping below the 50 SMA for the first time is often a significant bearish signal
- **200 SMA (long-term trend):** The dividing line between bull and bear. Stocks above the 200 SMA are in long-term uptrends. Stocks below it are in long-term downtrends. A gap that takes a stock from below to above the 200 SMA is especially significant

**Distance from SMAs:** A stock that's 30% above its 20 SMA is overextended. Even with a bullish catalyst, it may revert toward the mean. A stock near its 20 SMA has more room to run.

## 5.2 Key Levels

Mark these levels on your chart before the open. They are the lines in the sand where trades happen:

**Prior day high (PDH):** If today's stock breaks above yesterday's high, it's making a new high for at least two days. This is significant — buyers are willing to pay more than they did at any point yesterday. Trade: buy the break above PDH with confirmation volume.

**Prior day low (PDL):** If the stock breaks below PDL, sellers are in control. This is where "gap fills" target for gap-up-and-fade plays. Trade: short the break below PDL, or long at PDL as support for a bounce.

**Pre-market high (PMH):** The highest price traded in the pre-market session. If the stock breaks PMH at the open, it's very bullish — both pre-market and regular session participants are buying. Trade: buy the break of PMH, stop below VWAP.

**Pre-market low (PML):** If the stock loses PML, the pre-market bounce has failed. Bearish. Trade: short below PML, target yesterday's close.

**VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price):** The average price weighted by volume. Above VWAP = net buyers are profitable. Below VWAP = net buyers are underwater. This is the most important intraday level for institutional trading.

**Whole and half-dollar levels:** $10.00, $10.50, $20.00, $25.00, $50.00, $100.00. These are psychological levels where traders cluster orders. They act as magnets and inflection points. A stock struggling to break $50.00 after three attempts is telling you something.

**52-week high/low:** A stock at its 52-week high has no overhead resistance — every holder is profitable. A stock at its 52-week low has maximum overhead supply — every holder is underwater and may sell to "get out even."

## 5.3 Average True Range (ATR)

The ATR measures how much a stock typically moves in a single day, accounting for gaps.

**Why ATR matters for day trading:**

If a stock's ATR is $2.00 and it's already moved $1.80 from the open, it has likely used up most of its daily range. Entering a new position at this point has poor risk/reward — there's limited room to move in your direction, but plenty of room for a reversion.

**Practical applications:**
- **Stop distance:** Your stop should be within the stock's ATR. A $0.50 stop on a stock with ATR $0.40 is too wide — the stock doesn't normally move that much intraday. A $0.50 stop on a stock with ATR $5.00 is too tight — you'll get stopped out on normal volatility
- **Target setting:** A reasonable intraday target is 50-75% of the ATR. If ATR is $3.00, target $1.50-$2.25. Targeting $5.00 on a $3.00 ATR stock is unrealistic for a single day
- **Position sizing:** If ATR is $5.00 and your max risk is $250, you can only trade 50 shares ($250 / $5.00). If ATR is $0.50, you can trade 500 shares ($250 / $0.50). ATR automatically scales your position to the stock's volatility
- **Gapping assessment:** A stock with ATR $2.00 gapping $1.50 (75% of ATR) has used most of its daily move. A stock with ATR $2.00 gapping $6.00 (300% of ATR) is in outlier territory — something extraordinary happened

## 5.4 Historical Behavior After Gaps

Look at the last 5-10 times this stock gapped significantly. What happened?

**Some stocks are "gap and go" stocks:** They gap up and continue running. This tends to happen with high-growth stocks that get consistent good news — each gap is the market repricing fundamentally higher. Example: NVDA after earnings beats historically continues 60-80% of the time.

**Some stocks are "gap and fade" stocks:** They gap up and sell off to fill the gap by end of day. This tends to happen with stocks that frequently get hyped on social media or have a pattern of "sell the news" reactions. Example: many biotech stocks gap on trial results and then fade as reality sets in.

**Some stocks are "gap and chop" stocks:** They gap up and then go sideways for hours, frustrating both bulls and bears with no direction. These are the worst stocks to day trade — you tie up capital and mental energy for no result.

If a stock has faded its last 5 gaps, it's probably going to fade this one too, regardless of what the catalyst looks like. Behavior patterns persist because the stockholder base doesn't change quickly. The same institutions, algorithms, and retail traders who faded the last gap are still holding the stock and will react similarly.

## 5.5 Short Interest and Borrow Availability

Short interest tells you how many shares are currently sold short — shares that must eventually be bought back (covering), which creates potential buying pressure.

**Short interest ratio (days to cover):** Short interest / average daily volume. If a stock has 10M shares short and trades 2M/day, the days-to-cover is 5. It would take 5 full days of volume just for shorts to cover. High days-to-cover (>5) combined with a positive catalyst creates squeeze potential.

**Short interest as % of float:** More informative than absolute shares short.
- 5-10%: Normal, not significant
- 10-20%: Elevated. Worth noting. Squeeze possible on a strong catalyst
- 20-40%: High. Squeeze increasingly likely. These stocks move violently on positive news
- 40%+: Extremely high. Either the stock has genuine fundamental problems, or a squeeze is imminent. High risk either way

**Borrow availability:** Before you can short a stock, your broker must locate shares to borrow. If a stock is "hard to borrow" or has no shares available, you can't short it (or the borrow cost is extremely high). This information is available from your broker.

**The squeeze setup:** Low float + high short interest + positive catalyst + high pre-market volume = potential multi-hundred-percent move. These are rare but life-changing trades if caught early with proper sizing. They're also the trades where FOMO-chasers lose the most money entering late.

## 5.6 Sector and Sympathy Plays

Stocks don't move in isolation. They move in sectors. Understanding sector dynamics helps you identify opportunities and avoid traps.

**The sector leader:** When NVDA beats earnings and gaps 10%, it's the sector leader. AMD, AVGO, MRVL, and other semiconductor stocks will gap 2-5% in sympathy. The leader gets the biggest move and the most volume. Trade the leader, not the sympathy — unless you missed the leader and the sympathy has a better entry.

**Sympathy play rules:**
- The sympathy should be in the same industry (not just "tech")
- The sympathy should gap in the same direction as the leader
- The sympathy's volume should be elevated (confirming the sector rotation)
- The sympathy will typically make 20-40% of the leader's move
- Sympathy plays lose their edge quickly — if NVDA's earnings happened last night, the AMD sympathy play is over by 10 AM

**Sector ETFs as confirmation:** If XLK (technology sector ETF) is up 2%, the sector tide is bullish. Individual tech stocks swimming against this tide (gapping down while sector is up) have extra risk. Stocks swimming with the tide (gapping up when sector is up) have extra wind behind them.

## 5.7 The "Would I Sleep Holding This?" Test

Before entering any day trade, ask yourself: "If I had to hold this stock overnight, would I sleep soundly?"

If yes: your conviction in the catalyst and direction is genuine. You believe in the trade thesis, not just the chart pattern. This is a higher-quality trade.

If no: your conviction is weak. You're trading the chart, not the stock. You're one bad candle away from panicking. This trade requires tighter stops, smaller size, and faster profit-taking.

This test is not about whether you SHOULD hold overnight (you shouldn't — these are day trades). It's a psychological probe that reveals how much conviction you actually have. Low-conviction trades should be treated differently from high-conviction ones: smaller size, tighter stops, quicker exits.

The best trades are the ones where you'd be happy holding overnight but choose to close them because your system says to. The worst trades are the ones you're praying to break even on — those should never have been entered.
