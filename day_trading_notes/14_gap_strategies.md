# Chapter 14: Strategy 5 — Gap and Go / Gap Fade

## 14.1 Gap and Go

The gap-and-go is the most intuitive momentum strategy: a stock gaps up significantly, and you buy it expecting the move to continue.

**Setup conditions:**
1. Stock gaps > 4% pre-market on a Tier 1 or 2 catalyst (earnings, FDA, major contract)
2. Pre-market volume is heavy (>1M shares or >5x RVOL)
3. The gap is HOLDING in pre-market — the stock is near or above pre-market highs
4. The stock is above VWAP
5. There's a clear pre-market level to trade against (PMH)

**Entry:**
- Wait for the market open (9:30 AM)
- If the stock immediately pushes higher and breaks the pre-market high within the first 5-10 minutes, enter long above PMH
- Alternative: wait for the first 5-minute pullback after the opening push, enter on the first green candle

**Stop:** Below VWAP or below the opening 5-minute candle low (whichever is tighter)

**Target:**
- First target: +50% of the gap (if stock gapped 10%, target another 5%)
- Second target: whole number or round level above
- Trail remaining position below 9 EMA

**When gap-and-go works best:**
- First gap in a while (the stock hasn't gapped multiple days in a row)
- Catalyst is genuinely new information (not a rehash of old news)
- The sector is confirming (other stocks in the group are also up)
- The gap opens above prior resistance levels (no overhead supply)

**When gap-and-go fails:**
- The gap is into resistance (gapping into the 200 SMA, into a prior high)
- The stock has gapped 3+ days in a row (exhaustion)
- Pre-market volume fading into the open (early buyers are selling)
- No identifiable catalyst (the gap is random or social-media-driven)

## 14.2 Gap Fade

The opposite trade: a stock gaps up, but the gap is overdone relative to the catalyst. You short the stock, betting that it fades back toward yesterday's close.

**Setup conditions:**
1. Stock gaps > 4% but the catalyst doesn't justify the move (analyst upgrade, not earnings; social media hype, not FDA)
2. Pre-market shows weakness — the stock gapped to +8% but has faded to +5% pre-market
3. At the open, the stock immediately sells off (opening candle is red)
4. Volume is heavy on the sell side
5. The stock crosses below VWAP within the first 15 minutes

**Entry:**
- Short below VWAP after the stock fails to hold above it
- Or short below the opening 5-minute candle low
- Wait for confirmation — don't short a stock that's still above VWAP, even if you think it will fade

**Stop:** Above the opening range high or above VWAP + $0.20 buffer

**Target:**
- Previous day's close (the full gap fill)
- Or the prior day's VWAP
- Partial at 50% gap fill, trail the rest

**The 70% gap fill statistic:** Research shows that approximately 70% of gaps fill within 3 days. But the timing matters enormously. A gap that doesn't begin to fill within the first 30 minutes of the session is less likely to fill that day. If the stock holds above VWAP past 10:00 AM, the fade trade is dead.

## 14.3 Which Gaps Continue and Which Fade

This is the critical question. The answer depends primarily on the catalyst:

| Catalyst | Gap Behavior | Trade |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Earnings beat + guidance raise | Continues 70-80% of the time | Gap and go |
| Earnings beat, no guidance change | Continues 55-65% | Gap and go, but tighter stops |
| Earnings miss | Fades (bounces) 60% of time by day 2 | Wait for dead cat bounce, then short |
| FDA approval | Continues 80%+ | Gap and go |
| FDA rejection | Continues down 80%+ | Gap fade (short the bounce) |
| Analyst upgrade | Fades 55-60% same day | Gap fade |
| Contract/partnership | Depends on size; small = fades | Evaluate individually |
| Social media hype | Fades 65-70% | Gap fade (but dangerous — squeezes happen) |
| No catalyst | Fades 70%+ | Gap fade or skip entirely |
| Sector sympathy | Fades 60% (weaker than leader) | Gap fade after leader peaks |

**The key distinction:** Gaps driven by fundamental re-pricing (earnings, FDA) tend to continue because the stock is genuinely worth more/less than yesterday. Gaps driven by temporary demand (hype, technicals, sympathy) tend to fade because the underlying value hasn't changed.

## 14.4 The Pre-Market Level Method

A simple, mechanical approach to gap trading using three pre-market levels:

**Mark these levels before the open:**
1. **Pre-market high (PMH):** The highest price traded pre-market
2. **Pre-market low (PML):** The lowest price traded pre-market
3. **Yesterday's close (YC):** The prior day's closing price

**The rules:**

**Scenario 1: Stock opens above PMH**
- Extremely bullish. Gap-and-go. Enter long above PMH
- Stop at VWAP or PML
- Target: next resistance level or 3R
- This means the regular session opened HIGHER than the entire pre-market range

**Scenario 2: Stock opens between PMH and PML**
- Neutral to bullish. Wait for direction
- Break above PMH → long (gap-and-go)
- Break below PML → short (gap fade)
- Chop between → no trade

**Scenario 3: Stock opens below PML**
- Bearish. The gap is being sold. Gap fade candidate
- Confirm with VWAP break below
- Target: YC (full gap fill) or PML retest from below (failed support becomes resistance)

**Scenario 4: Stock opens below YC (gap reversed)**
- Very bearish. Stock gapped up pre-market but opened BELOW yesterday's close
- This is the "gap and crap" — all pre-market buyers are instantly underwater
- Short on the failed gap, target -3-5% from yesterday's close

## 14.5 Gap Fill Statistics

From academic and proprietary research across thousands of gaps:

**Same-day gap fills (gap fills completely within the same trading day):**
- Gaps < 2%: Fill same-day 75% of the time (too small to sustain)
- Gaps 2-4%: Fill same-day 55% of the time
- Gaps 4-8%: Fill same-day 35% of the time
- Gaps 8-15%: Fill same-day 20% of the time
- Gaps > 15%: Fill same-day 10% of the time (these are usually fundamental re-pricings)

**The "10:30 AM rule":** If a gap hasn't started to fill by 10:30 AM, the probability of a same-day fill drops by approximately 50%. A stock that's holding its entire gap past 10:30 AM is more likely to continue than to fade.

**Earnings gap fills:** Earnings gaps take longer to fill (if they fill at all):
- 40% of positive earnings gaps never fill within 3 months
- Earnings gaps that don't fill within 5 days rarely fill within 30 days
- This is why shorting earnings gaps is dangerous — you may be fighting a genuine re-pricing

**Practical application:** If you're trading a gap fade, give it until 10:30 AM. If the stock isn't fading by then, close the position. The gap is holding, and you're fighting a real move.

## 14.6 Risk on Gap Trades

Gaps create specific risk factors that don't exist in normal trading:

**The liquidity vacuum:** Between yesterday's close ($50.00) and today's open ($55.00), no shares traded. There are no support/resistance levels in this zone. If the stock starts to fade, there's no "floor" until yesterday's close. It can fall $5.00 with nothing to slow it down.

For gap-and-go trades, this vacuum is above you — if the stock reverses, you have $5.00 of air beneath you before the first support level.

For gap-fade trades, the vacuum works in your favor — the stock can fade quickly through the gap zone.

**The "hot potato" effect:** On high-volume gap stocks at the open, many traders are entering simultaneously. The stock is a hot potato — everyone wants to buy, hold briefly, and sell to the next buyer at a higher price. When the last buyer has bought, the music stops. The reversal is sudden and violent because the entire demand was speculative.

**Wide spreads at the open:** Gap stocks often have $0.10-0.50 spreads at the open. If you buy at the ask ($55.50) and the stock fades, the first sell price is the bid ($55.00). You're instantly down $0.50/share before the trade even has a chance.

**Mitigation:**
- Wait 2-5 minutes after the open for spreads to tighten
- Use limit orders, not market orders
- Size conservatively (0.5% risk instead of 1%)
- Have your exit plan BEFORE entering — don't figure it out while the stock is moving against you
