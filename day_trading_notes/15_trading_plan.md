# Chapter 15: The Trading Plan — Rules That Save You From Yourself

## 15.1 Why You Need a Written Plan

A trading plan is a contract between your rational self (the one reading this book at 8 PM, calm and clear-headed) and your emotional self (the one watching a position move against you at 10 AM with adrenaline pumping).

The rational self makes good decisions. The emotional self makes terrible ones. The plan ensures that the rational self is always in control, even when the emotional self is screaming.

If your plan is not written down, it doesn't exist. "I'll know a good setup when I see it" is not a plan. "I'll use common sense for my stop" is not a plan. "I'll take profit when it feels right" is not a plan.

A plan is specific, measurable, and leaves no room for interpretation:
- "I will only trade stocks on my watchlist"
- "My stop is $0.50 below entry, no exceptions"
- "I will exit 1/3 at 1R, 1/3 at 2R, and trail 1/3"
- "After 3 losses, I stop trading for the day"

Write it. Print it. Tape it to your monitor. Follow it.

## 15.2 Pre-Market Checklist

Complete this checklist every morning before 9:15 AM. If you haven't completed it, you don't trade.

**Market context (5 minutes):**
- [ ] Check overnight futures (ES, NQ): up, down, or flat?
- [ ] Check VIX: elevated (>20), normal (12-20), or low (<12)?
- [ ] Any major overnight news? (geopolitical, Fed commentary, earnings from mega caps)
- [ ] Any scheduled economic data today? (FOMC, CPI, NFP — check the economic calendar)
- [ ] Market bias for the day: bullish / bearish / neutral / no-trade (FOMC day)

**Scanner (10 minutes):**
- [ ] Run gap scanner: top 10 stocks by gap % with RVOL > 2x
- [ ] Verify catalyst for each scanner hit (earnings? news? nothing?)
- [ ] Check float and short interest for each
- [ ] Review pre-market price action (holding gap? fading? building volume?)

**Watchlist (10 minutes):**
- [ ] Narrow to 3-5 stocks maximum
- [ ] For each: mark PMH, PML, PDH, PDL, VWAP, key round numbers
- [ ] For each: define entry, stop, and target
- [ ] For each: calculate position size (shares = max risk / stop distance)
- [ ] Rank: #1 primary trade, #2-3 backup trades

**Self-assessment (2 minutes):**
- [ ] How did I sleep? (tired = reduce size to 0.5%)
- [ ] Am I carrying emotional baggage from yesterday's trading? (if yes, be extra disciplined)
- [ ] Am I trying to "make back" a recent loss? (if yes, reduce size)
- [ ] Is there anything in my personal life that would distract me? (if yes, consider not trading)

Total time: 27 minutes. If you're not willing to spend 27 minutes preparing, you're not serious about trading.

## 15.3 The Playbook

Each strategy has a specific playbook entry — an if-then structure that removes ambiguity.

**Example playbook card: ORB Long**

```
IF:
  - Stock gapped > 4% on Tier 1-2 catalyst
  - RVOL > 3x at 9:45 AM
  - Stock above VWAP
  - 15-min ORB high is below the daily ATR (room to run)
  - Market (SPY) is above its ORB high

THEN:
  - Buy above 15-min ORB high
  - Stop at ORB midpoint
  - Target 1: 1R (sell 1/3)
  - Target 2: 2R (sell 1/3)
  - Target 3: trail below 9 EMA (sell final 1/3)
  - Max hold time: 60 minutes (exit if target 1 not hit)

DO NOT:
  - Enter before the 15-min candle closes (wait for confirmation)
  - Chase if price is > 0.5R above ORB high (missed entry)
  - Take this trade if VIX > 35 (too choppy for ORB)
  - Take more than 2 ORB trades per day
```

Create a playbook card for every strategy you trade. The cards should be specific enough that someone else could execute them without asking you a single question.

## 15.4 Daily Risk Limits

Hard limits that cannot be overridden for any reason:

| Rule | Limit | Action When Hit |
|------|-------|----------------|
| Max risk per trade | 1% of account | Reduce shares, never increase stop |
| Max loss per day | 3% of account | Stop trading immediately. No exceptions |
| Max trades per day | 5 trades | After 5, stop regardless of P&L |
| Max consecutive losers | 3 in a row | Stop for at least 30 minutes; reassess |
| Max loss per week | 5% of account | Paper trade only for rest of week |
| Max drawdown from peak | 10% of account | Drop to 0.25% risk per trade until 50% recovered |

**The 3% daily loss limit is the most important rule in this entire book.** Without it, a single bad day can undo weeks of work. With it, even your worst day is manageable.

Enforce these limits mechanically. Some brokers allow you to set automatic loss limits that prevent order submission after a threshold is hit. Use this feature if available.

## 15.5 Weekly and Monthly Reviews

Trading without reviewing is practicing without feedback. You'll repeat mistakes indefinitely.

**Daily review (10 minutes, after market close):**
- Journal every trade (Chapter 16)
- Calculate daily P&L and R-multiple
- Identify the best and worst trade of the day
- Note any rule violations

**Weekly review (30 minutes, weekend):**
- Aggregate weekly P&L, win rate, average R
- Which strategies performed? Which didn't?
- Were there trades you should have taken but didn't? (missed opportunity analysis)
- Were there trades you took that weren't in the plan? (discipline analysis)
- Any patterns in the losing trades? (same time of day? same type of stock? same entry type?)

**Monthly review (1 hour, end of month):**
- Monthly P&L, Sharpe estimate, max drawdown
- Strategy-by-strategy performance breakdown
- Performance by time of day, by stock type, by market condition
- Are any strategies degrading? Should any be paused?
- Adjust rules if data supports it (not based on feelings — based on 30+ trades of data)

## 15.6 The A+ Setup Framework

Not all setups are equal. Grade each setup before entering:

**A+ (trade with full size and confidence):**
- All five confirmations present: volume, clean pattern, key level, catalyst, time of day
- Risk/reward > 1:3
- Strategy is performing well this week (positive expectancy)
- You feel calm and clear-headed

**A (trade with standard size):**
- Four of five confirmations present
- Risk/reward > 1:2
- Strategy is performing normally

**B (trade with half size or paper trade):**
- Three of five confirmations
- Risk/reward > 1:1.5
- Trade with reduced size ONLY if nothing better is available

**C (skip or paper trade only):**
- Two or fewer confirmations
- Risk/reward < 1:1.5
- Do NOT trade, regardless of how exciting the stock looks

**The discipline:** A+ and A setups should represent 80%+ of your trades. If more than 20% of your trades are B-grade, you're overtrading. If you're taking C-grade trades, stop trading and review your process.

The best traders don't take more trades than average traders. They take FEWER trades, of HIGHER quality. That's the entire edge.

## 15.7 When NOT to Trade

Knowing when to sit out is as valuable as knowing when to enter.

**Hard no-trade rules:**
- **FOMC announcement days (until 2:30 PM).** The market chops aimlessly until the announcement, then moves violently in both directions. Wait until 2:30 PM for the announcement, then assess
- **First 5 minutes of the session (9:30-9:35).** Too chaotic. Spreads are wide. Algorithms are firing. Wait for the noise to settle
- **Friday afternoon after 2:00 PM.** Volume dries up. Weekend position squaring creates false moves. The risk/reward of Friday afternoon trading is poor
- **After hitting your daily loss limit.** This is non-negotiable. Walking away from the screens after a losing morning is the hardest and most important thing you'll do as a trader
- **When you're tired, angry, or distracted.** Trading requires focus. If you can't give it 100% attention, you're bringing a knife to a gunfight. Sleep-deprived trading has the worst results of any condition
- **When the scanner shows nothing.** No quality setups = no trades. Not every day is a trading day. Some of the most profitable weeks include 1-2 "no trade" days where the savings from avoiding bad trades exceeds what you'd have made

**Soft no-trade signals:**
- VIX > 35 (everything whipsaws; ORB and VWAP strategies struggle)
- VIX < 12 (nothing moves; ranges are too tight to cover friction costs)
- Your best strategy has been losing for 3+ consecutive days (regime may have changed)
- Major holiday week (thin liquidity, unpredictable moves)
- You just had a large win yesterday (overconfidence leads to carelessness)

The market is open 252 days per year. You don't need to trade every one of them. Trading 150 days with A+ setups at full size will outperform trading 252 days with a mix of A+ and C setups. Less is more.
